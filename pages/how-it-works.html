How it Works

Resilience moves the goal from stopping every attack to keeping the business running through one. We organise every engagement around three layers.

  1. Layer 1 The cycle Anticipate, Withstand, Recover, Adapt
  2. Layer 2 The foundations Governance, risk management, minimum viable organisation
  3. Layer 3 The enablers Collaboration, leadership, culture

Layer 1

A continuous cycle, before, during and after an attack

Four stages that repeat. Each one feeds the next, so the programme gets stronger every time round.

  1. 01

    Anticipate

    Know which threats are most likely to hit your critical systems, and prepare before they arrive.

    What it takes to anticipate
    • Cyber risk intelligence and monitoring
    • Continuous threat exposure management
    • Threat modelling, including AI systems
    • Red, blue and purple team exercises
    • Blast-radius mapping of critical assets

  2. 02

    Withstand

    Keep critical services running while an attack is under way, and stop it spreading.

    What it takes to withstand
    • Layered defence and zero trust
    • Network and identity segmentation
    • Early detection with automated containment
    • Tested, isolated backups
    • Incident response retainer on standby

  3. 03

    Recover

    Restore operations quickly and safely, most critical services first, without destroying the evidence.

    What it takes to recover
    • Clean-room recovery from trusted images
    • Identity-first restoration of privileged access
    • Tier-ordered recovery against agreed downtime limits
    • Recovery and forensics in parallel
    • Business continuity plans and crisis communications

  4. 04

    Adapt

    Turn every incident and exercise into a stronger programme, so the same gap does not open twice.

    What it takes to adapt
    • Post-incident reviews that lead to actual fixes
    • Budget and staff moved to where incidents showed need
    • Retiring controls that no longer add value
    • Recovery rehearsals for regulators and customers
    • Scanning for upcoming regulation

Layer 2

Three foundations hold the cycle together

They sit at the centre of the cycle and decide who leads, what matters most and what gets protected first.

  • Enterprisewide governance

    Resilience is a shared responsibility, sponsored by the C-suite and overseen by the board. A steering committee and clear escalation lines keep oversight from slowing down a crisis response.

  • Cyber risk management

    Cyber risk sits inside enterprise risk management, with an agreed risk appetite, regular scenario exercises and baseline controls mapped to NIST CSF 2.0, ISO 27001, CIS 18 or NIST 800-53.

  • Minimum viable organisation

    Agree in advance the smallest set of functions the business needs to keep operating, then protect and recover those first. Everyone knows the priorities before the pressure starts.

Layer 3

What makes it work across the organisation

  • Cross-functional collaboration

    Security, IT, business continuity and enterprise risk work as one team.

  • Leadership buy-in

    Resilience is reported to the board in business terms.

  • A resilience culture

    Built into training, metrics and recognition at every level.
