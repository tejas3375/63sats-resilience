Understanding cyber resilience

What is cyber resilience?

It is your organisation's ability to keep delivering critical services during and after a cyber incident. It covers preventing attacks, but also detecting them quickly, limiting the damage and recovering in a planned way.

What do anticipate, withstand, recover and adapt mean?

They are the four stages of a continuous resilience cycle: anticipate threats, withstand attacks, recover quickly and adapt from what you learn. The cycle rests on three foundations: enterprisewide governance, cyber risk management and a defined minimum viable organisation. See how we apply each layer.

How does it relate to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework?

They work together. NIST CSF 2.0 (govern, identify, protect, detect, respond, recover) defines the security controls you need. The resilience cycle asks whether those controls keep the business running during and after an attack. We map your controls to NIST CSF and measure outcomes against the resilience cycle.

What is a minimum viable organisation?

The smallest set of business functions you need to keep operating through a serious cyber crisis. Agreeing it in advance, usually from a business impact analysis, tells every team what to protect and restore first, so those decisions are not made under pressure.

Planning, ownership and compliance

Why start with a business impact analysis?

Without one, response plans follow technical priorities rather than business ones, and recovery targets are often unrealistic. A business impact analysis ranks critical processes, maps their dependencies and sets recovery time and recovery point objectives agreed with the business. Security investment can then follow that ranking.

Should our CISO own business continuity and disaster recovery?

Usually not on their own, even though more security leaders are being asked to. Security is best placed to own cyber controls, monitoring and cyber incident response. Business continuity often fits better with operations or enterprise risk, and IT disaster recovery with infrastructure teams. A shared RASCI makes the split explicit.

Which regulations do you help with?

Common ones include CERT-In Directions, SEBI's cyber resilience framework, RBI guidelines, the DPDP Act and ISO 27001. We map controls and evidence to whichever apply to you.

What reporting will our leadership see?

Monthly posture reports written in plain language, showing the trend your leadership needs to see. Findings from every assessment sit in one risk register, ranked by business impact and tracked to closure.

Working with 63SATS

How is this different from a managed security service?

A managed security service usually focuses on monitoring and alerts. A resilience programme also covers governance, testing, response and recovery, with one team accountable for the outcome.

Do we have to replace our existing security tools?

No. We start from what you already run, integrate it where possible and only recommend changes where there is a clear gap.

Can we start with a single service?

Yes. Many clients begin with an assessment or a penetration test and extend into monitoring and response once they see the findings.

Is this suitable for small businesses?

Yes. Pre-configured packages give smaller teams the same lifecycle coverage without hiring in-house security staff.

Which industries do you work with?

Financial services, government and public sector undertakings, healthcare, manufacturing, global capability centres and IT services, and small businesses. The risks, rules and reporting lines differ by industry, so the programme does too.
