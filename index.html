Security and resilience answer different questions

Dimension Traditional security Cyber resilience
Core question Can we keep attackers out? How fast can we keep operating when they get in?
Measured by Threats blocked, tools deployed Time to detect, contain and recover; business impact avoided
Owned by The IT or security team The board, leadership and every business function
Scope Point products for individual risks One programme from governance through recovery
Priorities Every asset protected to the same standard Critical business services first, ranked by a business impact analysis
Proof Controls deployed and policies on file Defences tested against real attack paths, and recovery rehearsed

Start with what the business cannot lose

Protecting everything equally spreads budgets thin and leaves critical services exposed. We begin with business impact, then test, assign and report against it.

  1. Rank what matters

    We use your business impact analysis, or help the business build one, to sort systems into mission-critical, business-critical and the rest. Business owners set recovery time and recovery point objectives they can live with.

    You get: a tiered list of critical services and a defined minimum viable organisation.

  2. Test against real attackers

    We start from the threat actors active in your sector, rank each technique as probable, plausible or possible, and test the likeliest paths in depth. Each path is scored on whether it was prevented, detected, contained and recovered from in production, not on paper.

    You get: evidence of which defences hold, and which fail, on the attacks most likely to reach you.

  3. Make ownership explicit

    Resilience spans security, IT, business continuity, risk, legal and communications. We help you agree who is responsible, accountable, supporting, consulted and informed for each activity, so no one finds the gaps mid-incident.

    You get: a resilience RASCI and a steering group with clear decision rights.

  4. Report in business terms

    Leadership sets the tolerance, we show what it costs to meet, and if that is too high you decide together what to accept. Each decision records the exposure before and after, the evidence, the cost and the owner.

    You get: board reporting tied to revenue, service and regulatory outcomes, not tool counts.

  5. Rehearse and adapt

    Tabletop exercises for executives, crisis teams and technical staff, plus full recovery drills. Lessons go straight back into response plans, business continuity plans and the impact analysis itself.

    You get: tracked time to detect, respond and recover, and a falling count of open gaps.
